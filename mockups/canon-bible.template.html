1. What changes, at a glance

  • Gone: Marlow, the Lantern, the Hush, the Snuffer, the lullaby prologue with its long tapping climb, the Keeper's logbook, and the name Eko. I propose repurposing none of them (see decision 8).
  • New: Orbin and Helion, with faces and limbs; the Stillness; the guardian at the galaxy's edge, on the horizon from the first frame; your opening exactly as written; a playable memory of Helion after each world; a hub on the planetoid where Orbin fell.
  • Kept and reframed: the four planets, their inhabitants and their gifts; stars as the one currency; the ambush; the three-phase fight on Royalty; practice, checkpoints, B-sides; the Afterglow look and the "interface lives in the world" rule.
  • Kept as built: every gameplay system. The opening's "gravity path" and "Star-Nodes" are the orbit and the white marks you already play, re-dressed (section 5).

Why the last version failed, as I read your playtest: it asked for a minute of slow taps before anything was at stake, about characters with no faces, in a story told in asides. Your opening fixes all three in thirty seconds: one figure alone, one thing he wants, one tap that changes the whole screen. Everything below is built to protect that.

3. The cast

Orbin

the protagonist · golden, the smaller twin

A little fragile; his light shows everything. A thin orbit round his head with one bead on it, and a long plume from his crown that streams when he flies and hangs when he is low. He leans into everything and overshoots.

His eyes are like sunspots: small, dark, narrow, rimmed in ember. No mouth. Their slant and their lids carry the feeling.

Helion

his twin · the warm one, sun-like

Taller by a head, broader in the mantle, deeper orange, with a corona behind his head and a bigger heart of light. Steady where Orbin is eager; dry, teasing, kind. He keeps the beat for both of them, and counts.

"By a heartbeat. I counted."

The Stillness

a shadow with no shape and no tempo

It is painted as what it does, not as a creature: a dark with nothing in it, a broken rim that never repeats, and whatever it has reached held mid-motion and doubled, as if the picture had skipped. Near it the music stutters and loses its beat.

The guardian

at the galaxy's edge · World 1's last trial

Colossal, kneeling, made of the same dormant star-stone as the worlds, with broken orbits for shoulders and one great dull Star-Node in its chest. No face: a helm with one slit. It was this galaxy's keeper; the Stillness stopped it mid-watch and wears it as a gate. It is the largest fallen star of all, and it is reignited, not destroyed.

On animation, plainly. These are painted by code, and every part is a dial: lean, plume, eyelids, eye direction, each arm, the heartbeat. So they move smoothly and expressively by blending between poses (a flinch, a reach, a glance back), at 60 frames a second. It is not hand-drawn frame animation; if you want that, it is an animator's work on top of these designs. Faces read in story scenes and at the hub; in play Orbin is about 30 points tall, and there his posture and plume do the talking.

Making the first tap the best moment in the game

  • Nothing is loaded on the tap. The song is decoded, the chord is a ready buffer, and the coloured world and the shockwave are already painted while the scene plays, so the tap only starts things. The prompt does not appear until all of that is ready.
  • Sound starts on the tap itself, scheduled on the audio clock, not on the next frame.
  • The song's first bar has no taps (it already has a count-in bar), so the bloom has four beats to play out before anything is asked.
  • It is measured on a slowed processor as the last pass was, and it is the first thing I will ask you to try on your phone.

6. The sleeping worlds

World 1 is Galaxy 1: The Slumbering Drift. Every world in it is a fallen star, dim and frozen mid-note. Each keeps its song, its inhabitant, its gift and the gift's four beats as built.

One world's arc (Emberwick): you arrive to grey stone and a figure stopped mid-strike. The first bars are quiet. On the drop's big press the strike finally lands: Ossa wakes, colour floods, and it shows the Meteor once before it is yours.

What a first clear gives

One short celebratory sequence, about fifty seconds, each part skippable with a tap:

  1. The star reignites (6 s): the world's colour and song return. This is the Daybreak screen, already built.
  2. The gift (5 s): the inhabitant gives it, and Orbin uses it once in a small scene, so you see what you now have.
  3. The road (5 s): a stretch of the path to the galaxy's edge lights, and the guardian reacts.
  4. A memory of Helion (18 to 24 s, played).
  5. Home (5 s): the inhabitant arrives at the hub, and the hub's music gains its voice.

10. The road to the guardian

  • The map is one sky: the planetoid at the bottom, the four worlds along a road that climbs to the galaxy's edge, and there the guardian under the three shattered rings. It is the composition of the map you approved, repainted.
  • Each star reignited lights a stretch of the road, and the guardian reacts (table above). You always see how far there is to go.
  • The three rings are the three trials: one guardian a galaxy. When this one is reignited, the nearest ring breaks.
  • Gates as decided: a world opens with the one before it; the guardian opens on four worlds and 7 stars.

11. The Stillness in play

  • When it is near, the beat is lost: for a beat or two the music dulls and skips and the picture doubles, then both snap back on a downbeat. No tap is ever asked for during the skip, so every tap is still on a sound you can hear.
  • The ambush (after Cirra) is the Stillness reaching for the stars you have woken. The mechanics are the ones you playtested: four lights instead of windows, light notes that relight them, a checkpoint at each phrase, and the run ends if all four go dark.
  • Once in Verdance its shadow crosses the level, as planned.

13. Music, honestly

  • Helion's theme exists already. It is the music-box tune you chose for the old prologue. I would make it his: far and dark in the present, clear in the memories, and the ending theme. Nothing new to write.
  • The "rich chord". What I can make is a warm synthesised pad chord with a low swell and a shimmer. It will sound like a synth, not like strings or an orchestra. I cannot make convincing real strings, and I cannot hear what I make: I tune by measurement only.
  • So I made both versions for you to hear. In the game: the desk → for testing → Test chambers → Things to listen to. Helion's signal; The opening · with a synthesised chord; The opening · straight into the song. The first tap falls at 0:16 in both. The far music box, the note and the chord are in A-flat, which is what I measure the song's key to be.
  • A third way: a royalty-free orchestral swell you choose (Pixabay or similar). I can cut it to the song's key and tempo once you have picked it and I have checked its licence.
  • A Galaxy 1 theme: what I can do is the hub's own music growing voice by voice (the table in section 9), synthesised and simple. If you want a real orchestral theme, that is also a track to choose.

15. Decisions for you

  1. The twins' design: slender figures of light with small sunspot eyes, Orbin's head-orbit and plume, Helion's corona. Right direction, or closer to something else?
  2. The guardian: a kneeling colossus of star-stone with no face, reignited rather than destroyed. Yes?
  3. The camera: the low-angle shot is the opening's; play stays in the orbit view. Yes? (I recommend yes.)
  4. The first tap's sound, after you have listened: the synthesised chord, straight into the song, or a track you choose.
  5. The song starts on the tap itself (instant), not on the next pulse up to half a second later. (I recommend on the tap.)
  6. Memories cannot be failed. (I recommend yes: they are a reward.)
  7. The last memory plays before the guardian, not after. (I recommend before: it is why the fight matters.)
  8. Marlow, the Lantern and the Hush are dropped entirely; the rooms become the crater, Ossa's fire, the sky from the rim and the still pool; the sync check becomes listening for Helion. Yes?
  9. Names: the worlds and inhabitants keep theirs (Emberwick and Ossa, Cirra and Seo, Glassmere and Vell, Verdance and Morrow). The hub needs one: I suggest the Landing.
  10. The title writes itself at the end of Emberwick's first clear; later launches open on it. Yes?
  11. The lines: the ones in sections 2 and 7 are my proposals for the brothers' voices. Change any of them.
  12. Royalty for the guardian. (I recommend keeping it.)
