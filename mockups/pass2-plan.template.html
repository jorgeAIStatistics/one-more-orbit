3. The star collection

  • Our own star. Not a five-pointed character: a Star-Node's star, risen. A small hot heart with eight uneven rays and one thin orbit tilted round it in the world's colour, like the one round Orbin's head. Every world's star has its own orbit colour.
  • The sting. A short rising flourish of Helion's theme on a music box over one warm chord, written in each song's key and timed to start on the catch. I make it by synthesis and cannot hear it; you judge it in the listening room before it goes in.
  • The camera turn is one slow quarter-turn of the sky round Orbin during the burst, eased in and out. It is painted layers moving at different speeds, not a 3D camera.
  • Replays get the quick version: the star is already in the sky, so a small echo of it rises, Orbin touches it, and the results line is written. No flight home.
  • The count. The star you catch is the world's main star. The other four per world (shards in one flight, daredevil, every node on the beat, the far side) stay as they are, smaller.

4. The Landing as a resting place

Only the mood is taken from your reference: nothing in it is reproduced, and it stays in the Afterglow hand, not pixel art. Storyboard quality here; the finished painting is made in step 2.

  • Two lights. Cool starlight from one pale cluster high in the sky lies over everything. The fire is the only warm thing in the picture, and everything near it is lit by it. Helion's signal on the horizon is the same warm colour, kept small and far: the fire is where you are, the signal is where you are going.
  • The palette is deep blues, teals and night greens, with that one warm accent.
  • Five layers, each hazier and cooler: flowers and grass; Orbin and the fire; the Sleeper; mist; the horizon with the guardian, the three rings and the signal. They slide past each other gently whenever the camera moves.
  • The fire is made of the stars he has brought home. A tiny ember on the first visit. Each star sits small in its heart, in its own world's colour, and the fire is larger and warmer for every one. Flowers and grass come back round it, nearest first.
  • The Sleeper: the planetoid itself. An old being of stone, asleep since before the Stillness. The hill behind the fire is its bowed head and shoulder; the three great fingers of rock round the fire are its hand, cupped. It has no face: only one long closed seam of soft light where an eye would be, which opens a little with every star. It never speaks. It is not a tree, and it is nothing like the reference's.
  • Quiet motion: sparks rising, motes drifting, mist sliding, flowers swaying. Nothing loud.
  • After each star the flight home ends with a short quiet beat here (about three seconds: Orbin settles, the new star drops into the fire, the fire answers) before the map can be used. Replays skip it.
  • The sound is fire crackle and night air with a soft music-box version of Helion's theme, crossfading in and out of the levels. I synthesise all three and cannot hear them: you judge them in the listening room.
  • The title can be this same scene: Orbin asleep by the fire, the name written in the sky above the horizon, and the first tap wakes him and the camera rises to the map. On a first launch the cold open still comes first.

How this fits the map: the Landing is the bottom of the same painting the camera rises out of. Tapping the Landing on the map brings the camera back down to the fire, where its places are (the still pool, the rim, settings by the fire).

5. One visual language

  • One signal: a gold ring that closes on a star and touches its edge on the beat. Every tap in the game wears it: a node on the path, an orb in the air, a pad, a gate, the drop's great node. No ring, no tap.
  • Gold is the tap's alone. Danger stays vermilion. A hold is a calm blue ribbon. Everything else (the path, rings, particles, Orbin's trail, the core) is in quiet blues that cannot be mistaken for it.
  • Only the next two taps are lit. Nodes after them are small unlit stones until their turn. A node goes dark again the moment it is tapped.
  • Optional gold notes: I recommend removing them as separate targets. Flow is then earned by tapping the nodes themselves on the beat, which is what you are already doing. It removes the single biggest source of “is that one mine?”. The alternative (keeping them but drawing them unmistakably different) still puts a second kind of target on the course.
  • The star stretch is a quiet dotted line well outside the path, with a small star beside it. Nothing extra on the course.
  • At the drop the spectacle goes into the sky and the inhabitant, behind the course. The number of marks does not rise.

You can try it now on the current levels: add ?look=2 to the address (with ?fresh: ?fresh&look=2). It is switched off by default until you approve it.

8. Smooth, and fun to touch (built in step 2)

  • The picture is drawn between simulation steps (interpolated), the camera follows on eased springs, and a form change morphs over about 150 ms instead of popping.
  • Orbin gets anticipation before a hop, squash on landing, stretch at speed and a small follow-through, on weighted arcs.
  • Every tap answers in the frame it lands: a pitched note from the song's key, the node's star bursting, the ring snapping shut. An early or late tap shows a small tick before or after the node, with no penalty beyond flow.
  • Frame times are logged through the busiest drop and reported with the worst frame. If anything has to give, effects go before smoothness. I can measure this on a slowed processor; only you can measure it on your iPhone.
