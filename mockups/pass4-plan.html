4. Animation: in-betweens, not pose swaps

Today Ossa is one painted figure in two poses (lying, standing) that are swapped whole; the Sleeper and the star sequence do the same. The rebuild:

  • Cutout rigs, painted in the same hand: Ossa as a body, neck, head, two antler forks, four legs with a knee each, and a short tail, each turning on its own joint; the Sleeper as a head, a seam, and a hand of four fingers and a thumb; the Kindling as a body, two pairs of wings and a trailing wisp.
  • The principles: anticipation before a move (Ossa sinks before it rises, rears back before it strikes), follow-through and overlapping action (the antlers, tail and the Kindling's wisp lag behind the body and settle after it), slow-in and slow-out on every motion, movement on arcs, secondary motion (embers shaken off its back on each strike).
  • A living idle for everyone: breathing, the rib-fire's slow flicker standing in for a blink (Ossa has no eyes), small weight shifts. Nothing is ever perfectly still.
  • States as motions: asleep → waking → standing → hammering → celebrating, each change animated over 0.4–1 s, never a crossfade.
  • Orbin keeps his anticipation, squash and stretch; his trail flows. The camera follows on soft springs; speed and mode changes ease in.
