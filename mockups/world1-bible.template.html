1. What changes, at a glance

  • First launch is a playable prologue, not a title screen. The title is written into the sky when the prologue ends.
  • Every planet has a soul, a gift and one idea. The form a level introduces is that planet's gift to Eko, given in the level itself. It is played out in four beats (introduce, develop, twist, climax) and then retired until the finale.
  • The Lantern becomes a hub that relights room by room, with a logbook that gains a chapter per clear and an original theme that gains a voice per planet.
  • Stars become the one currency. Five ways to play each planet, one star each. The dark planet opens on the four planets plus a few stars.
  • An ambush after the second planet, and Royalty rebuilt as a three-phase fight with the Hush.

One reversal to be aware of. The last pass made earlier forms come back on later planets (the Meteor on Cirra, and so on). "A gimmick is then retired, returning only in the final level" undoes that: each planet keeps to its own gift. What still grows from planet to planet is the shared language: pads, orbs and combos, gates, twins. I recommend the new rule; it is decision 1 below.

2. Premise and tone

A small galaxy keeps time by a lighthouse. The Lantern's lamp hums, every planet hums back in its own colour, and the humming is what holds the dark apart. Its keeper, Marlow, a lampmoth, once struck the wick too hard; a spark came off it and did not go out. He named it Eko, because it answers.

Before the lamp there was only the quiet. The quiet has a shape, the Hush, and it has not slept since the humming began. One dusk it comes. It takes every planet's song, and their colour goes with it. It takes the keeper too, and leaves the lamp with one spark on its wick.

The game is that spark carrying four songs home, and then going up to fetch its keeper.

The Hush is not a monster. It is a neighbour who cannot sleep. The ending is not a killing: it is given the quietest song there is, the lullaby from the first minute of the game, and at last it sleeps. That turn is what the logbook quietly prepares.

Tone rules

  • No faces, no speech bubbles. Characters are light, posture and silhouette. Words are a few italic lines written into the picture.
  • Nobody explains a feeling. Marlow counts, complains about the glass, and says "go". The player does the rest.
  • Humour is dry and short. One line per scene at most.
  • Music tells the plot. The Hush has no voice; its presence is the song being pressed flat.

3. The cast

Eko

the spark · the player

A flame with a seed of light and a ring he wears. Never speaks. His character is in how he moves: he leans into everything, overshoots, and looks back for Marlow.

Each gift changes his trim and his trail, so the finale's Eko visibly carries all four.

Marlow

the Keeper · a lampmoth

Old, exact, dry, kinder than he lets on. He taught Eko to fly by counting. Under glass on the Hollow he still taps the beat: one, two, three, four.

"With the beat, little spark. Not before it."

The Hush

the Hollow · the quiet itself

Curtains of fog hung from above and, in its hood, an eclipse. It cannot bear a sound and keeps the songs in jars on threads. It wants rest, not ruin.

It never speaks. When it is near, the music is muffled.

Ossa

Emberwick · Heroes Tonight

A great slow beast of cooled slag with a fire banked in its chest and antlers like tuning forks. The smith who cast the Lantern's first glass. Gruff, proud, owes nobody.

"Take it. It does not stop for walls."

Sèo

Cirra · Sky High

Tall, thin, all angles, made of folded sailcloth. Keeps kites, which are how Cirra sings. Restless, starts sentences and abandons them, laughs first.

"Turn when the line pulls. You'll feel it. Probably."

Vell

Glassmere · Blank

Lives on the other side of the water and is seen only as a reflection, standing under whoever stands above. Formal, precise, very dry.

"You look well. From here, everyone does."

Morrow

Verdance · Invincible

A huge slow climber with a mossed back where the seed-lights live. Says little and means it. The oldest thing in the galaxy after the quiet.

"Hold on. Now let go. Both are the work."

The Snuffer

the Hush's hand · the ambush

A brass cap on a long crooked pole, fog hanging from its mouth, a sliver of the eclipse inside. It rides the Lantern's beam and puts lights out, one window at a time.

Never speaks. Beaten, it leaves its cap behind.

4. The four planets

Each planet is one short story told three times in a few seconds: a painted scene before the level (what is wrong), the soul appearing during it (at the gift, and in the drop), and a payoff after the first clear. Then a few words from Marlow along the Lantern's beam, and the next planet stirs.

Emberwick · the first gift

What is wrong. The forge-hills used to ring. Without the song the fires went out, and Ossa lies banked like a dead coal.

During. The first eight bars are the flying lesson continued: hops, gold, one spring pad, one planet hop. On the drop's downbeat the big press strikes Ossa's anvil-hill and it wakes. It shows the dash once, then it is yours.

After. Colour floods the sky (the Daybreak results). Ossa's forks ring; it gives Eko the coal to keep.

Verdance · the fourth gift

What is wrong. The canopy lights are out. Morrow let go, and sleeps at the foot of the tallest tree.

During. The gift is the Comet: hold to climb, let go to fall. Morrow climbs as you do; each seed-light it passes comes back on. Once, in the first drop, the Hush's shadow crosses the level: for two beats the music is pressed flat and the colour drains, then both snap back on the downbeat. Every tap is still on a sound you can hear.

5. The ambush

  • When. On the way home from Cirra's first clear. It cannot be skipped the first time; it is 48 seconds long.
  • What happens. The Snuffer comes down the beam putting out the Lantern's windows. You fight it off with both gifts so far: the Meteor through the fog it hangs in your way, the Satellite between its threads.
  • Light notes. Each four-bar phrase ends on one wide bright press. On the beat, the cap is knocked back up the beam and a window relights. A short roll before each charges the blow, as before a drop.
  • It previews the finale: the same light notes, the same song family, a first sight of the eclipse inside the cap.
  • Stars: one for beating it, one for doing it without losing a window.

Music. I propose a 32-bar cut of Royalty that the finale does not use, so the Hush has a theme you hear again at the end. That needs your ear: I have never heard the tracks, only measured them. It is decision 5.

6. The finale

Which song

Royalty is the fight; Invincible stays the fourth planet.

  • Its shape is already three acts: a 24-bar verse and break, a first drop, one bar of silence, a second drop.
  • It is the fastest and tightest level (160 BPM, ±80 ms), and already charted with every form and the Hush's red tethers.
  • Invincible is the right last planet: slow, heavy and swung, which is what a hold-and-let-go gift needs, and its title is the mood to walk into the Hollow with.

The ending (about 35 seconds, skippable). The lullaby from the prologue returns. The Hush, small after all, settles beside the lamp and sleeps. Colour floods the galaxy planet by planet. Marlow trims the wick. "One more orbit, Eko?" Then, far off on the horizon, another lamp, unlit, under a light that is not ours. "That one is not ours." A beat. "…Shall we?"

The finale is teased all the way: the Hollow grows on the map with every clear; Marlow's messages weaken; the ambush; and the Hush's shadow crossing Verdance.

The secret level

All twelve shards mend the Lantern's lens, and through it you can see a hidden moon. I recommend it be the lullaby itself, played at last as a level: original music, about 70 seconds, every gift, almost no sound but your own taps. No new licensed song is needed. (The alternative is a sixth track of your choosing; decision 6.)

  • The hub theme is original music in four voices, one per soul, built on the lullaby's harmony. I recommend this over layering the planets' real songs, which are in different keys and tempos and would clash; each planet's own song is still heard, muffled, when you drift near it.

  • The next planet always teases itself: its silhouette in the beam, its song through a wall, a glimpse of its soul.

11. One more try

  • A crash restarts at once, as now.
  • A near miss is said, briefly: from 80%, or within two bars of your best, the sky reads "so close: 87%" for a second as the run restarts.
  • Practice is offered, never pushed: after five crashes in the same four bars, one quiet line, "practise this part?", that fades if ignored.
  • Story never blocks a returning player: every scene skips on a tap; the prologue plays once and lives in the logbook after.
  • The results end on the hook, not on numbers: the Daybreak screen's last line becomes the next planet stirring.

  • On character animation, plainly: this gives expressive posture and light, not hand-drawn frame animation. If you want the second, that is an animator's work on top of these designs.

13. Nine decisions for you

  1. Retire each gift after its planet (undoing the reuse from the last pass)? I recommend yes.
  2. Royalty as the fight, Invincible as the fourth planet? I recommend yes.
  3. The Hollow's gate: four planets and 7 stars, or four planets only? I recommend 7.
  4. Prologue music: an original synthesised lullaby (which then returns as the ending and the secret level), or a cut of a licensed song? I recommend the lullaby; you would judge its sound in step 2.
  5. Ambush music: another cut of Royalty, or something else? Needs your ear.
  6. The secret level: the lullaby as a level, or a sixth song you pick?
  7. The fight restarts from the start of its phase in the main run (Daredevil is the whole thing in one go)? I recommend yes; it is 96 seconds at the tightest window.
  8. Fold quests and badges into stars? I recommend yes.
  9. The names (Ossa, Sèo, Vell, Morrow, the Snuffer) and Marlow staying "he": change any freely.
