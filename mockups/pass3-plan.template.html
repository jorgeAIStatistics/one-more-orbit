Emberwick keeps the Meteor: Volcano is staccato almost everywhere (a clap, off-beat stabs, a four-on-the-floor kick, the double hit); its only held sounds are the two risers (bars 8 and 16), so a hold-to-rise glide would have almost nothing to follow, and a dash answers a hammer. Rules for every level from here: one demand at a time (never a speed change, a mode change, a turn and a gravity change within the same 2 beats); a mode lasts at least a phrase and changes on a section change; when speed goes up, spacing goes up, the camera eases out, and there is always at least 1.5 s of course in view; turns are rare, announced a bar ahead and eased; the one gold signal stays.

3. Companions: three designs

Children of each reignited star. One of each kind lives by the fire at the Landing; in a level, a companion is the mechanic: it becomes the mode portal and gives Orbin its shape, or folds itself into a launch and throws him. Each is met in a short moment before its planet's level, so the new mode is a gift. None is star-shaped, none has eyes.

  • A. The Kindlings: a drop of the star's own fire with two pairs of moth wings made of light. Personality by flight, flicker and the way they circle the fire. Emberwick's gives him its heat (the Meteor's fire, or wings of heat if glide goes there); Verdance's would lift him (glide).
  • B. The Orrlets: a pebble of starlight with its own small moons. They act by opening their orbit: the ring he flies through is the mode portal, and spinning their moons up makes a launch ring. The most natural fit for a game about orbits; the plainest to look at.
  • C. The Bellseeds: a seed pod like a small hanging bell, one upright seam of light where it sings. Its note is the mode change, so each one adds a sound layer to the music; it opens like a flower to throw him.

My recommendation: A, the Kindlings. Moths come to a fire: they make sense living by the Landing's fire, they read clearly at a small size, and their wings can wrap Orbin for the half-second transformation without hiding the next tap.

4. The vessel: each star powers something you can see

The Sleeper is the vessel, and the Landing is its deck. Along its back are six sockets, one for each of World 1's great stars (the planetoid's own, the four worlds, the guardian's). The flight home ends with the star flying into the next socket: a power-up sound, the socket taking the star, light running down the old channels in the stone into the hand that holds the fire, the fire answering, a Kindling settling by it, and a new path lighting on the map.

  • First star (left): one socket lit, one channel of light, the seam where its eye would be barely open.
  • Five stars (right): five lit, the channels joined into a constellation along its spine, the seam open, mist running off the edge of the hand, and the line of a keel showing under the moss: it is about to rise.
  • Six: it wakes and carries Orbin off the planetoid toward Helion's signal. That is the end of World 1 and the bridge to World 2.
