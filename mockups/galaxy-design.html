ONE MORE ORBIT: the galaxy design

One touch. Follow the song. Bring the music back. Easy to start on the first planet; by the last one, a game of timing measured in a few hundredths of a second.

Where this starts from. Your brief describes the game as four levels. Since then the live game gained holds on every level, three stars per level, the World 1 map, B-side moons, quests, and a fifth level (Royalty) with tethers. This design keeps all of that and gives it a world; nothing is thrown away. Section 8 lists what is re-dressed and what is new. Six things need your decision; they are in section 9.

1. Story

At the middle of a small galaxy stands the Lantern, a crooked lighthouse on a rock. Its lamp does not only shine: it hums, and every planet hums its own song back. Marlow, an old lampmoth, keeps it lit. Eko is the youngest spark of that lamp.

One night the Hush drifts in: a moon-sized quilt of fog that cannot bear noise. It stitches each planet's song into a jar, cracks the Lantern's lens, takes Marlow so the lamp can never be relit, and settles on the Hollow, the dark planet at the edge of the map.

Eko slipped out through the crack. One spark, one beat. Each planet Eko clears gets its song and its light back, and the Lantern's beam gains that planet's colour, until the beam is strong enough to reach the Hollow.

Ending. The Hush is not destroyed. Unstitched, it turns out to be small and very tired. Marlow gives it the quietest song in the galaxy, and it falls asleep beside the lamp.

3. Gates, pads and chimes

  • Tempo gates: the orbit runs at 0.5×, 0.75×, 1.25×, 1.5× or 2× (today only 0.8× and 1.25×).
  • Flip gate (hops go inward instead of outward) and reverse gate (the orbit turns round): both exist today as bar rules; they become visible gates.
  • Mirror gate: the whole view flips left to right mid-song (today only the B-sides are mirrored, from the start).
  • Telegraph rule for all gates: they sit on a bar line, are on screen for the full look-ahead, the change is eased over half a beat (already built), and the first press after one has the wide ±160 ms window.
  • Springboard (no tap): throws Eko two beats through the air to a far ring or the next planetoid. Today's planet hop, without the press.
  • Tide pad (no tap): flips which way Eko hops, like a flip gate but placed on the ring.
  • Chime (tap): hangs between two rings. Tap as Eko passes through it to hop again without landing.
  • Slipstream (tap): tap on it and Eko is carried along its arrow for one beat.

Combos put these on consecutive notes of a hook: hop, chime, slipstream on three notes in a row. On planets 1 and 2 chimes and slipstreams appear only on optional shard branches; from planet 3 they are part of the main course.

Rules that stay: the same music always gives the same pattern; survival presses only on clearly audible strong beats; the build-up is a charge where nothing can end the run; restarts are instant; practice restarts from the section you reached; the foreground is drawn last on a clean band.

5. Mastery: shards, moons and badges

  • Lens shards replace today's stars: three per planet, fifteen in all. They are the pieces of the Lantern's cracked lens. Today a star is "play this stretch clean". A shard sits on a branch: a dotted gold path that leaves the safe line for two to four beats of harder presses in that planet's form, then rejoins.
  • A shard never blocks or ends anything. Miss a branch press and Eko simply stays on the safe line. A shard is kept only when that run clears the planet (as stars are today). Stars already in a save become shards.
  • What shards do: all three on a planet reveal its moon (the B-side, as today); they unlock Glows; all fifteen mend the lens and add a last frame to the ending.
  • Three badges per planet, shown as three pips on its card: Restored (cleared in one run from the start; practice checkpoints never count), In Tune (every flow note on the beat, 100%), Lens Mender (all three shards). Today's badges and quests stay.

6. Galaxy map, screens and look

Flow: title (the galaxy, with Eko circling the Lantern) → galaxy map → planet card → play. Moving between screens is one camera flight through the same scene, not a cut.

  • Look: a storybook galaxy. A deep indigo sky instead of black, round illustrated planets with soft glows, cream text, a rounded display typeface bundled with the game, gentle bobbing and blinking. Play keeps its clean band and its colour rules.
  • Kept and restyled: pause, results, settings, badges, quests, the sync check, and the orbs screen as Glows (the colour of Eko's ring and light).
  • Each planet's look follows its song: the planet sits at the centre of the rings (above), with its own sky, background shapes and drop set piece.

7. Boss fight: the Hush

On the Hollow, to Royalty (see decision 1). Nothing is random: every attack is written to the song, lands on a beat, and is answered with the one button. The Hush has a health bar in three segments, one per phase.

  • Counterattacks. After each attack pattern the Hush breathes in and its stitches loosen for two bars. The gold notes in that window are light notes: each one hit on the beat takes health off the current segment and makes Eko glow brighter.
  • Surviving always wins. The press that ends a phase removes whatever is left of that segment, so flow notes still never decide whether you finish. If the segment was already empty, the phase is a clean break: that is how the Hollow's three shards are earned.
  • Practice starts at any of the three phases.
  • Victory: the jars open, the Hollow floods with the four planets' colours, Marlow's jar lifts, and the song plays out over the ending cards.

8. What is re-dressed and what is new

Already in the game, renamed or re-dressed: hops, the two note layers, the charge and the drop, instant restart, practice by section; stars (become shards); B-side moons; quests; the map (becomes the galaxy map); planet hops (springboards); weave (Satellite); the drop's smash (Meteor); flips, turns and tempo changes (gates); twin orbit (dual); tethers (the Hush's threads); orbs (Glows); badges.

New to build: Pulsar and Comet; Meteor and Satellite as full forms with lenses and safe introductions; chimes, slipstreams and tide pads; the mirror gate and the wider tempo range; dual with two different forms; shard branches; per-planet windows; the world itself (characters, story cards, title, galaxy map, restyled screens, the planet at the centre of play); the boss fight.

9. Six decisions for you

  1. The boss song. I recommend Royalty, not Invincible. It is already the last level, it is the fastest track (160 BPM), its sections split cleanly into three phases, and its tethers become the Hush's threads. Invincible stays planet 4, where every form meets. The other way round also works: Invincible as the boss, Royalty as planet 4.
  2. Comet uses holding. You asked to be checked with. I recommend keeping it: the live game already asks you to hold on every level (rails and lines), so Comet gives holding a face instead of adding a new kind of input.
  3. What opens the Hollow. Today the gate needs 9 stars. Your brief says collectibles never block progress, so the Hollow would open when the four planets are cleared, and shards do the things in section 5 instead.
  4. Dual outside Invincible. You earlier ruled that the twin orbit stays in Invincible. This design uses it once more, in the boss's last phase. OK to lift that rule for the boss only?
  5. Tighter windows. Planet 1 gets easier (±140 ms); Verdance and the Hollow get clearly harder than today (±90 and ±80 ms, from ±120 and ±100). These are starting numbers to tune after your playtest.
  6. Names and looks. Eko, Marlow, the Hush, the Lantern, Emberwick, Cirra, Glassmere, Verdance, the Hollow, lens shards, Glows. Change any of them now and it costs nothing.
